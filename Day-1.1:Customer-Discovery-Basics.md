**Day 1.1: Customer Discovery Basics**

Core idea: an FDE should not jump directly into coding. First, understand the real customer problem.

Customer may say:

```text
We need an AI dashboard.
```

But the real problem may be:

```text
Managers do not trust sales reports because data is delayed, incomplete, and spread across multiple systems.
```

So before designing, ask questions in this order:

```text
User -> workflow -> pain -> data -> systems -> constraints -> success metric -> version one scope
```

Important discovery questions:

```text
Who uses this workflow every day?
What is the current process step by step?
Which step is slow, manual, risky, or error-prone?
What data is required?
Where does the data live?
Who has permission to access it?
What metric should improve?
What is the smallest useful version?
```

Interview answer to practice:

```text
I handle ambiguous requirements by first understanding the workflow instead of jumping to a solution. I ask who the users are, what process they follow today, where the pain is, what data and systems are involved, and how success will be measured. Then I define the smallest useful version, confirm assumptions with stakeholders, and build a prototype that can be tested with real users.
```

Your practice task for today:

```text
Customer request:
We need an AI dashboard for sales performance.

Write:
1. 5 discovery questions
2. possible real business problem
3. first prototype scope
4. one success metric
5. one risk
```
